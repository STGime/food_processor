# The Only Pizza Dough Recipe You'll Ever Need

Crispy outside, chewy inside, with big bubbly charred spots. This no-knead overnight dough takes 5 minutes of hands-on time and produces better pizza than most restaurants.

## Why Overnight?

Time does the work that kneading does. A long, cold fermentation in the fridge:
- **Develops gluten** without kneading — the flour hydrates and the proteins align naturally
- **Builds flavor** — slow yeast activity creates complex, slightly tangy notes
- **Makes it easier to stretch** — well-rested dough is relaxed and cooperative

## The Recipe

Makes 4 individual pizzas (about 250g each).

**Ingredients:**
- 500g bread flour (or "00" flour if you can find it)
- 350ml cold water
- 10g fine sea salt
- 1g instant dry yeast (about ¼ teaspoon — yes, that little)
- 1 tbsp extra virgin olive oil

**Equipment:**
- Large bowl
- Plastic wrap or a lid
- Sheet pan or pizza stone

---

## Method

### Day 1 — Mix (5 minutes)

1. In a large bowl, combine flour and salt
2. Add the yeast to the cold water and stir briefly
3. Pour the water into the flour and mix with a fork or your hand until no dry flour remains
4. Add olive oil and fold it in — the dough will be shaggy and sticky, and that's perfect
5. Cover with plastic wrap and put it in the fridge

**That's it for day 1.**

### Day 2 — Shape and Bake

1. **Take the dough out** 2 hours before you plan to bake. It should have roughly doubled and look bubbly.

2. **Divide into 4 pieces.** Flour your surface lightly and tip the dough out. Use a bench scraper or knife to divide into 4 equal portions.

3. **Shape into balls.** For each piece, pull the edges toward the center and pinch to seal, then flip seam-side down. Cup your hands around it and drag it in small circles on the counter to create surface tension. Place on a lightly oiled tray, cover, and rest for 2 hours at room temperature.

4. **Preheat your oven** to its maximum temperature (usually 250-275°C / 480-525°F) with a pizza stone or inverted sheet pan inside. Let it heat for at least 30 minutes.

5. **Stretch the dough.** Flour your hands. Pick up a dough ball and let gravity start stretching it. Use your knuckles to gently stretch from the center outward, leaving a slightly thicker rim. Aim for about 25-30cm (10-12 inches). Don't use a rolling pin — it presses out all the gas.

6. **Top simply.** Transfer to a floured peel or parchment paper. Add sauce, cheese, and toppings. Less is more — overloaded pizza = soggy pizza.

7. **Bake for 6-10 minutes** until the crust is puffed, charred in spots, and the cheese is bubbling.

## Topping Combinations Worth Trying

**Margherita (the classic)**
- San Marzano tomato sauce (just crushed canned tomatoes + salt)
- Fresh mozzarella, torn
- Fresh basil (add after baking)
- Drizzle of olive oil

**White Pizza**
- Ricotta + mozzarella
- Garlic
- Lemon zest
- Arugula (added after baking)

**Spicy Honey**
- Tomato sauce
- Mozzarella
- Spicy salami/soppressata
- Hot honey drizzle after baking

## Troubleshooting

**Dough won't stretch / keeps springing back:**
It needs more rest time. Cover it and wait 15-20 minutes, then try again. Gluten that's too tight hasn't relaxed enough.

**Dough tears when stretching:**
You're going too fast or the dough is too cold. Let it warm up more at room temperature.

**Soggy middle:**
Either too many toppings, too much sauce, or the oven wasn't hot enough. Pre-heat longer and go lighter on toppings.

**Not enough char/bubbles:**
Oven needs to be hotter, or try broiling for the last 1-2 minutes. A pizza steel transfers heat better than a stone if you want to upgrade.

## Storage

- **Fridge:** Dough balls keep for up to 3 days. The flavor actually gets better on days 2 and 3.
- **Freezer:** Wrap individual balls tightly in plastic wrap, then a freezer bag. Thaw overnight in the fridge, then bring to room temperature for 2 hours before stretching.

## The Secret

The secret to great pizza dough isn't technique or special ingredients — it's patience. Mix it, forget about it, and let time do the work. The best pizza you'll ever make at home costs about $1 in ingredients and 5 minutes of effort.
