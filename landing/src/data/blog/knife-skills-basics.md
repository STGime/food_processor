# Knife Skills 101: The Only 3 Cuts You Actually Need

Forget fancy culinary school techniques. You don't need to julienne, chiffonade, or brunoise anything to cook great food at home. Master these three cuts and you'll handle 90% of home cooking prep work.

## Cut #1: The Rough Chop

**What it is:** Cutting ingredients into irregular pieces, roughly the same size.

**When to use it:** Anything that's going to be cooked down — soups, stews, sauces, stocks. Also for herbs you're adding to a blender or food processor.

**How to do it:**
1. Stabilize the ingredient on the cutting board (cut a flat side if it rolls)
2. Cut into roughly equal pieces — they don't need to be pretty
3. Aim for pieces within the same size range so they cook evenly

**The key insight:** Nobody will see these pieces in the final dish. Stop trying to make them perfect.

---

## Cut #2: The Dice

**What it is:** Cutting ingredients into small, even cubes — roughly 1cm (½ inch).

**When to use it:** Sautéed vegetables, salsas, stir-fries, grain bowls, anything where the pieces are visible in the final dish.

**How to do it (onion example):**
1. Cut the onion in half through the root
2. Peel it, keeping the root intact (it holds everything together)
3. Make horizontal cuts toward the root, stopping before you cut through
4. Make vertical cuts from top to bottom
5. Cut across to release the dice
6. Discard the root end

**The key insight:** Keep the root end intact — it's nature's handle. It holds the onion together while you cut.

---

## Cut #3: The Thin Slice

**What it is:** Cutting ingredients into thin, even slices — about 2-3mm thick.

**When to use it:** Garlic, mushrooms, radishes, cucumbers for salads, potatoes for gratins, anything that needs to cook quickly or look delicate.

**How to do it:**
1. Create a flat, stable surface by cutting a thin slice off one side
2. Place the flat side down
3. Use a smooth, forward-and-down rocking motion
4. Keep your fingertips curled under (the "claw grip") with your knuckles guiding the blade

**The key insight:** The claw grip isn't optional. Tuck your fingertips behind your knuckles and let the side of the blade rest against your knuckles. This is how you go fast without going to the hospital.

---

## The One Technique That Makes Everything Better

Regardless of which cut you're making, the single most important technique is: **keep your knife sharp.**

A sharp knife is safer than a dull one. A dull knife requires more force, which means less control, which means more slipping. Get a simple whetstone or a honing steel and use it regularly.

You don't need 12 knives either. A single good 8-inch chef's knife will handle everything above and more.

---

## Practice Makes Permanent

Don't practice speed — practice consistency. When all your pieces are the same size, they cook at the same rate. That's the whole point.

Start with an onion. If you can dice an onion well, you can dice anything.
